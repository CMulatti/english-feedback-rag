# SAMPLE: cadet_routine_07
Task: cadet_routine
Scores: Content 5 | Organization 5 | Language 5

## Text
Dear Jim,
Life in the M.A. is hard, but I like it. 
I wake up at 6.00 am, I take a shower, and I get dressed. Then, I line up in the Alpatacal Couryard and I have breakfast in the mess hall. We have bread with butter or cheese. After that, I go to the classroom. I line up and have lunch at 1.00 pm. After that, I have more lessons but sometimes we practice for ceremonies or do sports. We do a lot of PT in the M.A. For example, on Mondays I play basketball, and on Thursdays I do rucksack march. After dinner in the evening, I study in the library with the other cadets. Finally, I go to the barracks. I go to bed at 10.00 pm. 
Take care,
CDT [NAME]

## Teacher justification
- Content: Answers all of Jim's essential questions: says life in the M.A. is hard but he likes it, describes the whole daily routine, and explains which activities he does and how often ("sometimes", "on Mondays", "on Thursdays"). Free time is not mentioned, but as an accessory point this does not affect the message. Details like PT, ceremonies, the rucksack march and studying with other cadets show it is a cadet's routine and not a civilian's. The text is within the expected length. The target reader is fully informed: band 5.
- Organization: The text is connected and coherent. It uses a range of connectors ("Then", "After that", "Finally") and also other cohesive devices ("but", "For example") and time references ("in the evening", "on Mondays"), which make the flow of ideas clear. Punctuation is used consistently and makes the text easy to read. It follows the format of a reply, with a proper greeting and ending. This matches band 5: connected and coherent, using basic linking words and a limited number of cohesive devices.
- Language: Uses present simple consistently, with subjects in every sentence, and uses frequency expressions correctly. Includes a good range of taught vocabulary (line up, mess hall, classroom, library, barracks, PT, ceremonies, rucksack march). There are a few minor errors ("Couryard", "do rucksack march" instead of "do a rucksack march"), but they are noticeable without affecting meaning. This matches band 5: everyday vocabulary used appropriately and simple grammatical forms with a good degree of control.
